Case study / 2016
Master’s thesis

Urban Community Accessible Facilities Design Based on the Service Concept

A service-design study of everyday community facilities in Chengdu, exploring how physical facilities and the services around them could better support residents’ daily lives.

Year2016
ContextMaster’s thesis
Chongqing University
RoleProject execution & thesis writing
MethodsLiterature review · In-depth interviews · Participant observation

Background & design question

The study examined tensions and problems in the establishment and use of urban community convenience facilities. It considered these facilities as part of a wider service system, connecting residents, community organisations, property management and everyday activities.

Against a background of increasingly smart equipment, digitally supported services and simpler product aesthetics, the project explored how service-oriented design could make community facilities more useful and convenient for residents.

My contribution

I was responsible for carrying out the project and writing the master’s thesis, from preliminary research and field investigation to the analysis and development of design proposals.

Field research in Chengdu

Field visits covered three community case settings across different areas of Chengdu. Literature review, in-depth interviews and participant observation supported the initial investigation and collection of contextual material.

  1. Gaoxin community residents’ committee成都高新社区居委会
  2. Longfor Flamingo residential community and Longfor Century property management成都龙湖弗莱明戈小区及成都龙湖世纪物业
  3. Jun’an Weishi Garden community, Chengdu High-tech Zone成都高新区军安卫士花园社区

The investigation identified issues in how convenience facilities were established and used. These observations informed a re-examination of the relationship between facilities and their associated services.

From service touchpoints to design proposals

Touchpoint analysis was used to review and restructure the provision of community facilities and related services. This helped identify key opportunities for intervention and develop alternative design solutions.

01Investigate

Literature, interviews and participant observation

02Reframe

Facility use, service relationships and touchpoints

03Propose

Service-oriented redesign across four areas of daily life

Four areas of community life

The proposals addressed cultural, health, transport and commercial needs, with the aim of improving residents’ experience of everyday community services.

01 / Culture

Cultural life

Reconsidering facilities and services that support residents’ everyday cultural activities.

02 / Health

Health & wellbeing

Exploring community facilities that respond to residents’ health-related needs.

03 / Transport

Everyday mobility

Considering convenience facilities as part of local travel and movement.

04 / Commerce

Local services

Re-examining the facilities and service touchpoints involved in everyday commercial activities.

Design outcomes & intent

The project produced an analysis of facility-related service touchpoints, a restructured view of the service system and redesign proposals across the four areas. The intended contribution was a more people-centred community service environment in which facilities could better serve and benefit residents.

These outcomes are presented as research and design proposals; improvements in residents’ experience are project aims rather than reported post-implementation results.
